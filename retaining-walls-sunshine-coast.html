Retaining Walls Sunshine Coast Explained

Every indicative rate band in the retaining walls sunshine guide carries the same warning: the figures exclude GST, excavation, drainage, imported backfill, engineering and certification. That list is not fine print, it is the difference between a filter and a budget.

On a sloping block with tight access, excavation alone can move the final figure well beyond the face rate, and on taller walls the engineering and certification items are mandatory rather than optional. A sensible owner treats each band as a first filter: it rules options in or out before formal quoting starts, and no more. Once two quotes are on the table, the only fair comparison is one where both are read against the same named exclusions and the same drainage scope.

Surcharge: the detail that reclassifies your wall

The detail that most often changes a wall from a simple job into an engineered one is what sits above it. A low garden terrace has a contained brief, and the guide still places timber sleepers on that low duty work. A wall holding up a driveway, a shed slab or another loaded area carries a surcharge, and that single fact pushes the job toward design and certification.

The structural pathway is explicit: retaining walls are Class 10b structures designed to AS 4678, and an engineered wall is typically certified by an RPEQ engineer through a Form 15 for the design and a Form 16 on inspection. Sunshine Coast Council requires a building permit before construction, modification or repair of most retaining walls, and its own approval as a concurrence agency applies to walls over 2 metres. Licensing also matters less by trade name than owners assume, because QBCC licensing is triggered by the value of the building work, not by the height of the wall.

Face area arithmetic every owner should run first

Walls are priced by square metre of wall face, which is height multiplied by length. The guide's own example is the one worth memorising: a wall 1.2 metres high and 10 metres long is 12 square metres of face, not 10 metres of wall. A job that sounds short can still present a sizeable face area, which is why this single calculation should happen before any quote is requested.

Ground is the second half of the arithmetic. Blackall Range basalt clay, Buderim volcanic soil, Landsborough sandstone and coastal dune sand each behave differently, so the same material can price and perform very differently at Maleny compared with Maroochydore. That is also why the useful early question is not which wall type is popular, but why a given wall type suits the block in question.

Reading the drainage lines in a quote

Drainage sits behind every wall choice, and the source material treats it as structural rather than cosmetic. The build up it names is ag drain, aggregate and geofabric behind the wall face, and it states plainly that poor drainage is the leading cause of wall failure. Even naturally free draining options such as rock, boulder and gabion are still framed with a subsoil drainage requirement behind retaining work on this coast.

That framing changes how quotes should be read. A cheaper figure is not automatically a better one if the drainage detail is vague, omitted or shifted into a general allowance. If a quote names the drainage build up specifically, with the ag drain, backfill specification and outlets, that is usually the mark of a scope that has been thought through. The same logic applies to repairs: a failing, bulging or cracked wall should not be assumed to need only a cosmetic fix, and the published cost, ground and approval guide points owners toward deciding between repair, partial rebuild or full replacement before any finish is discussed.

Who this applies to

Not every reader arrives with the same job in front of them, and the rate bands mean different things depending on which of these situations describes yours.

  • Slope owners with a fall they cannot use, who need the height and surcharge position worked out before materials are argued about.
  • Replacement jobs on cut and fill blocks, where concrete sleepers between galvanised posts are described as the workhorse option.
  • Acreage owners with room behind the face, where a rock wall battered back into the slope can suit the ground.
  • Erosion prone sites, where gabion baskets address scour and surface runoff on erodible ground.
  • Owners of failing walls, who need a drainage and condition assessment before choosing repair or replacement.

What to put in the brief before quotes

The guide's starting position is that the first useful document is a brief, not a mood board. Four details do most of the work: the wall height, the length, the suburb or postcode, and what sits above the wall. Those four facts decide whether the job is a simple terrace, a replacement on cut and fill ground, or a structure already edging into engineering and permit territory, and they are enough to make early conversations specific rather than guesswork.

  1. Run the face area. Multiply wall height by length to get square metres of face. A wall 1.2 metres high and 10 metres long is 12 square metres of face, not 10 metres of wall.
  2. Record what sits above the wall. A driveway, shed slab or loaded area above the wall points to surcharge and the engineered pathway, while a garden bed points to a contained, low duty brief.
  3. Identify your ground. Note whether the block is basalt clay, volcanic soil, sandstone or dune sand, and record the suburb or postcode so early conversations are grounded in real conditions.
  4. Compare quotes on identical exclusions. Check that GST, excavation, drainage, imported backfill, engineering and certification are named consistently across each quote before comparing face rates.
  5. Interrogate the drainage detail. Ask for the ag drain, aggregate and geofabric build up behind the face to be specified, because poor drainage is the leading cause of wall failure.

Common questions

How is retaining wall cost calculated on the Sunshine Coast? Published bands are quoted per square metre of wall face, which is height multiplied by length, and range from about $150 to $300 per square metre for rock and boulder walls up to $550 to $750 for core filled block. Those figures exclude GST, excavation, drainage, imported backfill, engineering and certification, so the face rate is only ever part of the total.

When does a retaining wall need engineering and council involvement? Walls of one metre or more, or walls carrying a surcharge such as a driveway above them, typically move into the engineered pathway with AS 4678 design and RPEQ Form 15 and Form 16 certification. Sunshine Coast Council requires a building permit for most retaining wall work and acts as a concurrence agency for walls over 2 metres.

Why do quotes for what looks like the same wall differ? Because the exclusions differ. One quote may include excavation, drainage and certification while another leaves them as allowances, and drainage in particular is structural: the ag drain, aggregate and geofabric build up behind the wall is the detail most closely tied to whether the wall fails early or lasts.
